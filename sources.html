03 / What they started with

Nine shifts.
An open future

Participants treated these as changes already underway: the shared backdrop for the exercise. They were working assumptions, not guarantees.

Read the nine shared starting points
  1. Growth in the scale and speed of legal activity.
  2. Pressure toward outcome-based value and pricing.
  3. Greater reliance on data and evidence as a basis for trust.
  4. Deeper integration of human and machine workflows.
  5. Scalable legal capability alongside differentiated judgment.
  6. Movement from static documents to dynamic agreements and systems.
  7. A wider strategic and operational role for legal.
  8. Changing training, apprenticeship and entry routes.
  9. Pressure on institutions and regulation to keep pace.
Condensed from the Phase 1 synthesis
Scope and limits

What this account can tell us

Whose work this is

The workshops examined the future of the business of law to 2040 from the perspective of corporate legal, with implications for the broader legal ecosystem. This guide is Ken Priore’s independent presentation of the material, not an official organizer publication.

The scenario teams developed possible worlds. The later stakeholder groups discussed their responses to those worlds. The guide preserves that distinction.

What remains incomplete

The source set is strongest on the first workshop’s discussion and the later workshops’ outputs. It does not preserve every conversation, every participant’s position or a complete record of subsequent implementation.

Historical signal cards are described as workshop inputs. They are not presented as a current market or legal update. Source titles and locators are provided here; the underlying workshop archive is not republished in this guide.
